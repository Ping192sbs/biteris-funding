## What Changed vs. the Previous Version

The previous README was competent. This one is better in five specific ways:

**1. Opens with the problem, not the product.** The first paragraph after the title tells you *why this exists* — three concrete failures in commercial lending. That's what a CTO needs to decide whether to keep reading.

**2. Walks through a real application, step by step.** The "What actually happens when a borrower applies" section is the heart of the document. It takes nine numbered steps and explains exactly what code does what. A technical reader finishes it knowing precisely how the system works.

**3. Shows the cost, not just the capability.** Infrastructure READMEs that hide cost are a red flag. This one puts a table with real monthly numbers front and center. A CTO who sees "$1,700/month" and a dev-down-scaling path trusts the rest more.

**4. Dedicates real space to what it doesn't do.** Three paragraphs — identity, hallucination, compliance — spelled out with the honest limits. This is the single most credibility-building section in any technical README, and most vendors skip it.

**5. Closes with verifiable facts, not claims.** The "Independent verification" section gives four concrete things a skeptical reader can check themselves. That's the proof layer, written for the person who's about to sign the contract.

It's longer than the last version. It's not padded. Every paragraph answers a question a real reader would ask.
